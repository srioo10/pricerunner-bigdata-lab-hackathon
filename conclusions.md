# Conclusions

## Descriptive analytics
- The raw file was already tidy: no missing values and no exact duplicate rows. The noise is inside the titles. Cleaning removed 853 of 35,311 listings (2.42%): 41 titles with no informative word and 812 titles longer than the 1.5xIQR fence of 105.5 characters.
- Two cleaning mistakes only showed up in later steps and were fixed. German listings had lost their umlauts ('geschirrsp ler'), which produced a nonsense per-category rule {geschirrsp} -> {ler}; 1,987 fragments were repaired and the common words translated. The model-code filter also deleted spec words such as 1400rpm and lga1151 (2,359 occurrences), so it now keeps them. A few German compounds are still untranslated: the shortest Fridge Freezers rule below rests on 'gefrierkombination' (59 listings).
- Selection kept the title words and the category. Product ID is an identifier and Category ID is a copy of the category. Cluster Label is the product name, and its words are already in the titles. Cluster ID is kept only to build a test split with unseen products.
- The 10 categories are mildly imbalanced (Fridge Freezers 5,407 vs Freezers 2,195, ratio 2.46). Title length is close to symmetric. Listings per product are right-skewed (skew 1.73), and Box-Cox brings this down to 0.005. Brand (the first informative word of the title, used as a proxy) and token counts are heavy-tailed: 25 brands cover 70.3% of listings, and token frequency falls off with a Zipf slope of -1.53 over ranks 100 to 4,352.

## Predictive analytics
- Apriori at 1% support and 80% confidence finds 1,128 frequent itemsets and 3,542 rules, 465 of them with a category as the consequent. They show which words go with which category, but they were mined on all listings and are not a tested classifier. Brand-model pairs have the highest lift ({apple} -> {iphone}, lift 74.8). Going from 5% to 1% support makes Apriori about 5 times slower (0.73 s to 3.85 s, median of 3 runs). Without the category item the same settings give 570 itemsets and 997 word-to-word rules. Inside single categories, 5-10% support finds model rules the global run misses, such as {hobbs, microwave} -> {russell}.
- The decision tree reaches 94.7% test accuracy (95% bootstrap interval 0.9418-0.9524) and 0.949 macro F1. On products it never saw in training it scores 94.0%, and 93.9% +- 0.4 over 5 different product splits. The vocabulary size and tree settings were tuned on the ordinary training split, so the unseen-product figure is slightly optimistic. Chi-square selection did not help: cross-validation kept all 1,972 words. The tree beats both Naive Bayes baselines on the same features (92.6% and 93.1%), and McNemar's test says these gaps are not chance (p below 1e-7). Cleaning leaves accuracy where it was (raw titles give 94.8%) and gives a tree with 199 fewer leaves. Most errors are Fridges labelled as Fridge Freezers (67 test listings).
- The tuned tree has 1,219 leaves and depth 228. Cost-complexity pruning chosen by cross-validation cuts it to 426 leaves for a loss of 0.2 points, so most of the depth only memorises small groups of titles. For TVs, dishwashers, CPUs, microwaves and washing machines one product word plus a few absent words is enough (3 to 6 conditions, 1,500-2,200 training listings each). Freezers and Fridges need 21 conditions for leaves of about 40-50 listings, the same refrigeration group where the tree makes its errors.
- K-Means on TF-IDF/LSA vectors finds no natural number of clusters: silhouette stays between 0.046 and 0.160 for k from 2 to 24, and k=10 was used to match the categories. The run reported is the lowest-SSE one of 5 seeds, picked without the labels; it gives purity 0.722 and NMI 0.698, and NMI over the 5 seeds is 0.692 +- 0.015. Five clusters are at least 95% one category (microwaves, TVs, washing machines and two CPU clusters, one led by Intel terms (intel, core, ghz) and one by AMD and Hewlett Packard Enterprise server terms). The most mixed cluster (2,399 listings) gathers Bosch appliances from four categories (dishwashers and the three refrigeration types), held together by the words bosch and serie.
- In the dendrogram of category centroids, Freezers and Fridge Freezers merge first (0.340) and CPUs join last (0.876). Fridges joins that pair at the very next merge (0.383), and Fridges labelled as Fridge Freezers is the tree's most common error.
- MAFIA finds 156 maximal frequent itemsets at 1% support, against 695 closed and 1,128 frequent ones, and mlxtend's fpmax returns the same 156 sets. The longest maximal itemsets have 7 items and describe a whole product type, e.g. {4k, hd, hdr, smart, tv, ultra, cat=TVs}.

## PySpark (local multicore scaling)
- Spark gives the same results as the single-machine libraries. FPGrowth finds the same 1,128 itemsets with the same counts, and Spark's tree scores 0.875 against sklearn's 0.876 on the same feature recipe.
- On the data as given Spark is slower in every test (speedups 0.32, 0.22 and 0.57), because each Spark job pays a fixed start cost that a 34k-row file cannot pay back.
- With the data copied up to 20 times, Spark's time grows much more slowly. The decision tree is 1.12 times faster at 2x, 2.18 at 5x, 3.83 at 10x and 4.11 at 20x. FPGrowth on Spark beats mlxtend Apriori only at 10x (1.17) and stays below mlxtend's own FP-growth at every size (0.76 at 20x), so for association rules the gain comes mostly from the FP-growth algorithm.
- The Spark benchmark tree scores lower (0.875) than the tuned tree in Section 5 (0.947). The benchmark gives both engines the same simpler task: a fixed 1,000-word vocabulary built from the less-cleaned title text, depth 20, and its own 80/20 split.

## Limitations
- All timings come from one 16-core laptop in Spark local mode against single-threaded Python, so they show local multicore scaling, not a cluster. Across the 3 repeats a speedup moves by up to 0.89 (decision tree at 20x: 3.52 to 4.41), so only the clear gaps should be read; Section 8 gives every range.
- The larger Spark inputs are copies of the same data. They measure throughput only and say nothing about how the models would do on new products.
